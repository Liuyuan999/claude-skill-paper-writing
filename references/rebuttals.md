# Rebuttals and Responses to Reviewers

Respond to the concern that was raised. Do not invent additional objections or weaken the paper to sound agreeable.

## Build a comment ledger

For each comment, record:

- reviewer and comment anchor
- underlying technical concern
- direct answer
- evidence already in the paper
- new analysis or revision, if any
- manuscript location changed
- unresolved boundary

Combine duplicate concerns while preserving reviewer attribution.

## Response order

1. Give the direct answer.
2. State the theorem, derivation, experiment, citation, or correction that supports it.
3. Explain the manuscript change.
4. State a remaining boundary only when it affects the answer.

Use `Yes`, `No`, or a precise correction when appropriate. Do not begin every response with thanks or agreement. Thank a reviewer for a useful correction, missing citation, or experiment when the thanks carries information.

## Technical responses

- Quote exact theorem, equation, figure, table, or line references.
- Give the new result in the response rather than promising to add it later.
- Distinguish a clarification from a new experiment or changed claim.
- If the reviewer requests a different problem, explain the relation to the submitted problem without dismissing the request.
- Correct factual errors directly and politely with evidence.

## Style

Apply [writing-style.md](writing-style.md). Keep the response self-contained and compact. Avoid canned openings, fake concessions, defensive hedging, and repeated statements about what the paper does not claim.

Do not use reviewer dialogue in the manuscript unless a technical distinction is genuinely needed there. A rebuttal can answer an objection. The revised paper should usually state the corrected argument directly.

## Final check

- Every comment has a response or an explicit reason it cannot be answered.
- Every claimed revision appears in the manuscript.
- Every new number matches the source result.
- Response claims do not exceed revised paper claims.
- The strongest concerns appear first.
- The response remains readable to an area chair who has not reread the paper.

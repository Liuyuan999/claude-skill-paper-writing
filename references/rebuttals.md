# Rebuttals / Response to Reviewers

Based on the de-facto standard (Devi Parikh et al., "How we write rebuttals") plus general
ML/optimization practice.

---

## Twin goals: clarify and convince

The test: could a **neutral third party** tell that the concerns were addressed *purely
from your rebuttal*? Reviewers may have forgotten or misunderstood the paper — re-establish
context.

## Two audiences — don't neglect the AC

- **Reviewers** — may need clarification or correction.
- **The Area Chair (the judge)** — will likely read *only* the reviews + your rebuttal. A
  common beginner mistake is to write only to reviewers and ignore the AC. Make the
  top-level take-away legible to someone who hasn't re-read the paper.

## Process

1. **Itemize every comment in a spreadsheet** (early). Spot concerns common across
   reviewers, miss nothing, and identify any new experiments needed in time to run them.
2. **Brain-dump** rough responses — "being convincing and concise is a subtractive
   process."
3. **Draft** covering every point.
4. **Revise**, prioritizing major concerns within the space limit.

## Structure & prioritization

- **Start positive** — briefly recap reviewer-noted strengths so the AC remembers them.
- **Lead with the biggest concerns** you can answer well.
- **Quote the reviewer, then respond directly first** — "Yes." / "No." / "Not quite." /
  "We disagree with the premise, because…" — *before* adding context.
- **Consolidate** concerns shared across reviewers ("To R1 and R3 on …").
- **Color-code or label by reviewer** (R1/R2/R3) for scannability.

## Handling specifics

- **Stats/experiments over arguments.** A new number beats a paragraph of reasoning.
- **"Don't promise — do."** Give the explanation/result *in* the rebuttal, then say you'll
  add it to the paper. Don't merely promise to address it.
- **Self-contained.** Re-introduce acronyms/setup; cite exact line/table/figure numbers so
  reviewers get credit for details they missed.
- **Try suggested experiments** rather than only arguing against them; if the venue forbids
  new experiments, say so transparently.
- **Flag bad-faith or factually wrong reviews** explicitly and politely, with evidence.

## Tone

- Conversational, not combative — "these are your colleagues"; it's a scientific *and*
  social interaction.
- Address the reviewer's **intent**, not just the literal wording.
- Be receptive and reasonable; concede valid points gracefully.
- **Thank** reviewers for typos, references, and good suggestions.

## When a reviewer misunderstood

Prefer **fixing the paper** so the misunderstanding can't recur ("We have revised Section 3
to clarify …") over insisting "we already said this." Then point to where.

## Template

```
We thank the reviewers for their constructive feedback. We are glad R1/R3 found
<strength> and R2 noted <strength>. We address the main concerns below; minor points and
typos are fixed in the revision.

[Major concern A — raised by R1, R3]
> "<quote>"
Short answer: <Yes/No/Not quite>. <Evidence: new result / table ref / clarification>.
We have added <X> to Section <n>.

[Concern B — R2]
> "<quote>"
<direct response + data>.

[Minor points]
R2: <typo/ref> — fixed. ...
```

## Self-check

- Could the AC, reading only the reviews + this rebuttal, see each concern resolved?
- Did you answer each point directly *first*, then justify?
- Did you back claims with numbers/refs rather than assertions?
- Did you "do, not promise"?
- Is the tone gracious and the response self-contained?

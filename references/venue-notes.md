# Venue Notes

Use these as audience and emphasis guides, not rigid templates. Verify current official formatting, anonymity, checklist, and submission requirements before claiming compliance.

## NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR

- Make the technical contribution and evidence visible early.
- Keep the main claim consistent across abstract, introduction, theory, experiments, and conclusion.
- Give enough proof structure in the main paper to evaluate the result.
- Make experimental comparisons reproducible and budget-aware.
- Treat appendices as support, not as a place to repair an unclear main claim.

## AISTATS

- Emphasize statistical, optimization, and methodological precision.
- State assumptions, estimands, uncertainty, and finite-sample or asymptotic regimes clearly.
- Distinguish theoretical novelty from an application of known tools.

## ICASSP

- Narrow the paper to the result that earns the limited space.
- Prefer one coherent method and evaluation story over compressed background.
- Keep equations, algorithm descriptions, and experimental settings sufficient to interpret the result.

## IEEE-style journals and transactions

- Favor complete definitions, reproducible algorithms, and consistent notation.
- State the contribution relative to the closest journal and conference literature.
- Keep main claims, proofs, simulations, and implementation evidence synchronized across a longer manuscript.
- If extending a conference paper, state the technical delta precisely.

## Optimization and theoretical ML journals

- Make the problem class, oracle model, assumptions, and solution criterion explicit.
- Distinguish convergence from complexity and local from global guarantees.
- Give complete proofs and trace constant or parameter dependence that matters.
- Compare results under compatible assumptions and cost models.

## Applied ML papers

- Make the application question, data regime, baselines, and evaluation protocol precise.
- Separate a method contribution from a system or implementation contribution.
- Match broad claims to dataset diversity, scaling evidence, and statistical support.
